Organised by validity type (internal / external / construct / statistical-conclusion). Every row states the limitation, the specific claim it constrains, and how it is handled in this dissertation — several of these are reported *as results* (e.g. the memory contamination rate, the contextual-prefix regression) rather than hidden, and the "reporting" column points to where. This table is the honest counterweight to the results in §4 and feeds the reflective discussion in §5.3.

**Reading.** The pattern across this table is deliberate: the project is architecturally richer than its empirical coverage, so wherever a mechanism could not be validated at scale it is either (a) scoped down in the claim (rows 4, 6, 7) or (b) reported as an explicit negative result (rows 6, 11, 12) rather than omitted. The two threats a reader should weigh most heavily are **external validity** (rows 1–2: the system is demonstrated mostly in simulation, on one day of real data, in one room) and the **memory relevance-gate gap** (row 6: recall helps recurring incidents and harms novel ones by the same mechanism). Both are carried forward explicitly into the Conclusion and Future Work rather than being treated as solved.
